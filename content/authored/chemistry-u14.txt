The general formula of alkenes is: | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | CₙH₂ₙ | CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁ | B | e
The general formula of alkynes is: | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | CₙH₂ₙ | CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ | CₙH₂ₙ₊₄ | C
Alkanes are called saturated hydrocarbons because they contain: | only single bonds between carbon atoms | at least one double bond | a triple bond | no hydrogen | A
The simplest alkane is: | ethane | methane | ethene | ethyne | B | e
The molecular formula of an alkane with 5 carbon atoms is: | C₅H₁₀ | C₅H₁₂ | C₅H₈ | C₅H₁₄ | B | h
The molecular formula of an alkene with 3 carbon atoms is: | C₃H₈ | C₃H₆ | C₃H₄ | C₃H₃ | B | h
The molecular formula of an alkyne with 4 carbon atoms is: | C₄H₁₀ | C₄H₈ | C₄H₆ | C₄H₄ | C | h
Ethene (C₂H₄) contains a: | single bond only | carbon–carbon double bond | carbon–carbon triple bond | benzene ring | B
Ethyne (C₂H₂) is commonly called: | methane | acetylene | propane | butane | B
The gas used with oxygen in welding torches is: | methane | ethyne (acetylene) | propane | ethane | B | s
